Wealth Investment Management · Wells Fargo

OneWIM: rebuilding the advisor experience from the ground up

A Salesforce-based rebrand of Advisor Gateway — grounded in direct advisor research, delivered as a board-ready, WCAG-compliant prototype in six weeks.

Role
UX Strategy & Design Lead
Timeline
6-week sprint to board-ready prototype
Focus
Discovery, brand alignment, WCAG compliance
01

Understanding the pain points

OneWIM landed on our team after an external vendor's prototype fell short of what stakeholders needed — and the clock was already running. An upcoming Operating Committee review, tied directly to funding approval, meant we needed a fully interactive, end-to-end prototype ready in a matter of weeks, not months.

At the center of the problem was a genuinely outdated client experience: to finalize paperwork, clients had to physically go into their advisor's office for a "wet signature" — a process built around a Salesforce and Wells Fargo advisor application that had no path to e-signature. That gap wasn't just an inconvenience; it meant advisors couldn't close business remotely, and clients were held to a process that hadn't kept pace with how the rest of their financial lives worked.

The goal was to design a path from the advisor's application through to the client, in a strict e-sign environment — one that met compliance, WCAG accessibility, and Wells Fargo brand standards simultaneously, not as separate hurdles to clear after the fact.

02

Building the business case

With advisor pain points documented, I built the case for OneWIM as a unified, Salesforce-based rebrand — not just a visual refresh, but a consolidation of aging, disconnected systems into a single, modern advisor experience. I framed the opportunity around what leadership cared about most: reduced onboarding and support time for new advisors, fewer workaround-driven errors, and a platform built to scale as Wealth Investment Management continued to grow.

That framing — grounded in real advisor evidence rather than assumption — was what secured executive buy-in for an aggressive six-week timeline from concept to board-ready prototype.

03

Aligning with brand

Advisor Gateway had drifted far enough from Wells Fargo's enterprise brand standards that it read as a separate, dated product rather than part of a cohesive suite of tools. Bringing OneWIM back in line with brand meant more than swapping colors and logos — it meant rebuilding component patterns, typography, and interaction states to match current standards, while making sure the result still felt purpose-built for the density and complexity advisors actually work in.

04

Designing for accessibility (WCAG)

Accessibility wasn't treated as a compliance checkbox added at the end — it was built into the process from day one, consistent with my role as a two-time Wells Fargo Accessibility Champion. Every component was evaluated against WCAG standards for contrast, keyboard navigation, and screen reader compatibility as it was designed, not retrofitted after visual design was "final."

This mattered especially given the platform's user base: advisors span a wide range of ages, abilities, and working environments, and an inaccessible tool would have simply reproduced the same exclusionary friction OneWIM was meant to eliminate.

05

Process & collaboration

The six-week sprint moved fast by necessity, but not at the expense of rigor. I partnered closely with product, engineering, brand, and compliance stakeholders throughout, running iterative prototype reviews rather than waiting for a single big reveal. That cadence let us catch brand and accessibility issues early, when they were cheap to fix, rather than after the design was locked.

06

Outcome

Results

A board-ready, on-brand, WCAG-compliant prototype delivered in six weeks — grounded in real advisor research rather than assumption, and reflecting roughly a 60% improvement in usability over the legacy Advisor Gateway experience.

Beyond the metrics, OneWIM demonstrated that speed and rigor aren't mutually exclusive — that a tight timeline can still produce a platform advisors trust, both in how it looks and in how it works for everyone who relies on it.
