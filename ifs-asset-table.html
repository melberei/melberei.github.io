Investment Fiduciary Services · Wells Fargo

Reimagining data density in the IFS Asset Detail Table

Full visibility into 17 to 20 columns of investment data on tablet, without sacrificing a single data point, by challenging a "hard browser limitation" that turned out not to be one.

Role
Lead, IFS 1 & IFS 2 Teams
Platform
Tablet, high-density data table
Focus
Research, component reuse, data integrity
01

The constraint

When I took over the Investment Fiduciary Services (IFS) project at Wells Fargo, one of the most highly funded initiatives in the bank's history, I inherited a critical usability gap in the asset detail table. On tablet, relationship managers and clients could only see eight columns of data, despite needing access to seventeen to twenty. The existing rationale was that this was simply a browser limitation, that true responsiveness wasn't achievable at that breakpoint. I pushed back on that assumption.

02

The research

Leading both the IFS 1 and IFS 2 teams, I directed competitive research to identify how other platforms handled dense, high-stakes data on constrained screens. Part of that approach was digging into what competitors and other design teams were doing to solve large, complex tables without compromising the customer experience. After a fair amount of research on my own, I found detailed, well-documented examples on Shopify's Shared UX site and brought them to our stakeholders and front-end development lead.

03

The solution

Rather than building something new from scratch, we were able to reuse an existing component already in our design system: the paddle controls from our carousel pattern, adapted here to navigate table columns instead of carousel slides. We landed on a solution that kept a core set of columns visible at all times, while allowing users to navigate additional columns using those paddle controls paired with dot indicators, giving them a clear sense of how many columns remained and how many they'd already passed.

04

Outcome

Results

Full visibility into investment portfolios without sacrificing a single data point, even on smaller screens. Every column retained its totals row, so nothing about the integrity of the data was lost in the process, proving that "the browser won't allow it" was a design assumption worth challenging, not a hard limit.
